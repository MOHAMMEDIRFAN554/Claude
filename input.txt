Once upon a time, in a small village by the river, there lived a girl named Mira. Every morning she walked to the well with a clay pot on her head. The road was long and dusty, but Mira liked to sing as she walked. The birds in the trees would sing with her, and the old farmer by the gate would wave his hat.

One day, Mira found a small blue stone on the road. It was warm, and it glowed softly in her hand. She put it in her pocket and went to the well. When she lowered the pot into the water, the stone began to hum. The water in the well turned bright and clear, and Mira could see the sky inside it.

"What are you?" Mira asked the stone. The stone did not answer, but it hummed a little louder. Mira smiled and said, "Then you will be my friend." From that day on, the water in the village was always clean, and the people were happy.

The farmer asked Mira, "Why is the water so sweet now?" Mira laughed and said, "It is a secret, but it is a good secret." The farmer nodded and went back to his fields.

Once upon a time, there was a little robot named Bolt. Bolt lived in a big city full of tall towers and bright lights. He was very small, and the other robots often walked past him without saying hello. Bolt wanted to have a friend more than anything in the world.

Every night, Bolt climbed to the top of a tower and looked at the stars. "Maybe there is a friend for me up there," he said. One night, a tiny light came down from the sky and landed next to him. It was a little star, lost and far from home.

"Can you help me get back?" the star asked. Bolt thought for a moment and said, "I will try." He built a tall ladder from old metal and wire. It took him many days and many nights, but he did not give up. At last the ladder touched the sky, and the star climbed home.

"Thank you, Bolt," said the star. "You are a true friend." After that, every night the star shined a little brighter over the city, just for Bolt. And Bolt was never lonely again, because he knew that a friend was always watching over him.

Once upon a time, a young boy named Arjun wanted to learn how to fly a kite. His grandfather gave him a red kite with a long tail. "The wind is your teacher," said his grandfather. "Listen to it, and it will show you the way."

Arjun ran across the field, but the kite fell down. He tried again, and it fell again. He was sad and wanted to stop. His grandfather sat beside him and said, "Every bird falls before it flies. Try one more time." Arjun stood up, held the string, and waited for the wind.

When the wind came, Arjun let the kite go. It rose high into the sky, higher than the trees, higher than the hills. Arjun laughed and shouted, "Look, grandfather, it is flying!" His grandfather smiled and said, "No, my boy. You are flying."

That evening, Arjun and his grandfather walked home under the orange sky. The kite was tired, and so was Arjun. But his heart was full, and he dreamed of flying all night long.

Once upon a time, a clever fox lived at the edge of a deep forest. The fox was very proud of his long red tail. He liked to tell the other animals that he was the smartest creature in the land. The owl only listened and said nothing.

One day, the fox saw a bunch of grapes hanging from a high vine. He jumped and jumped, but he could not reach them. "Those grapes are sour anyway," he said, and he walked away. The owl watched from her branch and laughed softly.

The next day, a little mouse came to the vine. She did not jump. She climbed up the stem, one small step at a time, and ate the sweetest grapes in the forest. The fox saw her and felt ashamed. The owl said, "Being smart is not about talking. It is about thinking."

From that day on, the fox talked a little less and listened a little more. And sometimes, when the grapes were ripe, the mouse shared them with him.

Once upon a time, in a kingdom by the sea, there was a queen who loved books. She had a library with ten thousand books, and she read one every night before she slept. But the people of the kingdom could not read, and the queen was sad.

So the queen opened the doors of her library. "Anyone may come," she said. "I will teach you myself." At first only a few people came. A fisherman, a baker, and a small girl with no shoes. The queen taught them letters, then words, then stories.

Soon the fisherman taught his sons, and the baker taught her friends, and the small girl taught everyone in her street. Within a year, the whole kingdom could read. The library was full every day, and the people told stories in the market and by the fire.

The queen grew old, but she was happy. "A book is a door," she said. "And now every door in my kingdom is open." When she died, the people wrote her story in a book, and they put it in the library for everyone to read.

Once upon a time, a little turtle named Tiko wanted to see the ocean. He lived in a pond far away, and the ocean was many days from home. His friends said, "You are too slow. You will never get there." But Tiko only smiled and began to walk.

He walked through the grass and over the hills. He walked in the rain and in the sun. When he was tired, he rested under a leaf. When he was hungry, he ate a little green plant. Every day he went a little farther, and every day he was a little closer.

One morning, Tiko heard a sound like a thousand drums. He climbed a sandy hill, and there it was, the ocean, blue and wide and shining. The waves rolled up to his feet and said hello. Tiko laughed and said, "Slow is not the same as never."

Tiko lived by the ocean for many years. And when young turtles asked him how he got there, he always said the same thing. "One step, then another step. That is the whole secret."

Once upon a time, a baker named Rosa made the best bread in the town. Every morning before the sun came up, she lit her oven and mixed flour, water, salt, and a little honey. The smell of warm bread filled the streets, and people lined up at her door.

One winter, a poor old man came to the bakery. He had no money, and his hands were cold. Rosa gave him a warm loaf and a cup of tea. "Come back tomorrow," she said. The old man came back the next day, and the next, and every day that winter.

In the spring, the old man did not come. Rosa was worried. Then a letter arrived. It said, "Thank you for the bread and the kindness. I was once a baker too. Here is my secret recipe." Rosa tried the recipe, and the bread was even better than before.

From then on, Rosa gave one free loaf every day to anyone who needed it. "Bread is for sharing," she said. "Kindness always comes back, like the smell of bread in the morning."

Once upon a time, there was a cloud named Puff who did not want to rain. "If I rain, I will become small," said Puff. "I want to stay big and white and beautiful." So Puff floated over the fields and kept all of his water inside.

Below him, the fields grew dry. The flowers bent their heads, and the farmers looked up at the sky with worried faces. A little girl called out to Puff, "Please, cloud, we need your rain." Puff felt sad, but he was afraid.

Then the wind said to Puff, "When you rain, you do not disappear. You become the river, and the river becomes the sea, and the sea becomes a cloud again." Puff thought about this for a long time. At last he let his rain fall.

The fields turned green, the flowers lifted their heads, and the little girl danced in the rain. Puff became small, then smaller, then he was gone. But a few days later, a new cloud rose from the sea, big and white and happy. It was Puff, and he was home.

Once upon a time, a girl named Lina found an old key in her garden. It was made of gold, and it had a small star on the top. She looked for a door that the key would open. She tried the front door, the back door, and the door of the shed, but the key did not fit.

Lina asked her mother, "What does this key open?" Her mother smiled and said, "Some keys open doors. Some keys open hearts." Lina did not understand, so she kept the key in her pocket and waited.

One day, a new boy came to her school. He sat alone and did not talk to anyone. Lina felt the key in her pocket, and she walked over to him. "Hello," she said. "Do you want to play with me?" The boy looked up and smiled for the first time.

That night, Lina told her mother about the boy. Her mother said, "You see? You found the door." Lina took out the key and looked at the little star. It seemed to shine just a little brighter than before.

Once upon a time, an old lighthouse stood on a rock in the middle of the sea. Every night its light turned round and round, and it showed the ships the way home. The lighthouse keeper was an old woman named Nora, and she had kept the light burning for fifty years.

One stormy night, the light went out. The wind was too strong, and the rain was too heavy. Nora climbed the long stairs in the dark, one step at a time. Her legs were tired, and her hands were cold, but she did not stop.

At the top, she lit the lamp with her last match. The light flashed out across the dark water. Far away, a small fishing boat saw the light and turned toward home. The sailors cheered, and the boat came safely into the harbor.

In the morning, the sailors came to thank Nora. "You saved our lives," they said. Nora only smiled. "The light saved you," she said. "I only kept it burning." And she went to sleep, while the sun rose over the quiet sea.

Once upon a time, a small elephant named Gopi was afraid of water. All the other elephants loved to splash in the river, but Gopi stayed on the bank and watched. "What if I sink?" he asked his mother. His mother said, "Elephants do not sink. We are made for the water."

Gopi put one foot in the river. It was cold, and he pulled it back. He put his foot in again, and this time he left it there. Then he put in another foot, and another, until he was standing in the river up to his knees.

A little fish swam by and tickled his leg. Gopi laughed so hard that he fell into the water with a big splash. He waited to sink, but he did not sink. He floated, and he kicked his legs, and he swam. "I am swimming!" he shouted. "Mother, look at me!"

From that day on, Gopi was the first elephant in the river every morning and the last one out every night. And when other young elephants were afraid, Gopi always said, "Put one foot in. The water is your friend."
